Hello, dear friends. As you know, in the previous lesson, we signed up for Databricks free edition and logged into the platform. In this lesson, we will explore the Databricks user interface step-by-step. Yes, friends, when you first open it, the screen may look a little complicated because there are many different menus and options. But don't worry. I will show you what each section does in a clear and simple way. For now, this will just be a brief introduction. We'll only learn theoretically what each option is for. Remember, we'll go into more detail in the following lesson. So let's get started without wasting time. Friends, let's focus on the left side of the screen. Here, you'll see a long menu bar. This menu is the gateway to all the features Databricks offers. Everything from opening a notebook, uploading and processing data to building machine learning models starts from here. If you pay attention, the menu is divided into three main categories. SQL, Structured Query Language, the language used to query data. Data Engineering, the process of collecting, cleaning, and processing data. AI slash ML, Artificial Intelligence slash Machine Learning, the process of building models with artificial intelligence and machine learning. Depending on the area you're working in, you'll focus on the relevant menu. For example, in this course, we'll mainly deal with the Data Engineering section. Now let's go through the menu bar in more detail, starting from the top. The first button you'll see is the New Plus button. This means start something new. From here, you can quickly create a new notebook, a new folder, or a new job. Notebook is just like a sheet of paper. You can write code in it, run SQL queries, and visualize the results. Right below that, you'll see the Workspace menu. The workspace is your personal working area. You can think of it like folders on your computer. This is where you store your notebooks, code files, and data sets. There is also a shared workspace area. Here, you can collaborate with your teammates on shared files. For example, if you upload a data cleaning script for a project here, everyone in the team can access the same file. Just below the workspace, you'll find the Recents section. This part lists the files you recently opened. Let's say yesterday you opened a notebook called Test Run. If you want to open it again today, you don't need to search. It will be listed under Recents right away. Another important menu is the Catalog section. The Catalog is where you organize and access your data. Here, you'll find tables, schemas, and catalogs. They may not be visible right now, but as we progress, you'll see these elements in action. Yes, you can think of these like this. Table, like a sheet in Excel made up of rows and columns. Schema, a structure that groups tables together like a folder. Catalog, a larger folder above schemas. The main framework that organizes all your data structures. With a catalog, you can manage your data securely and in an organized way. For example, if you work at an e-commerce company, you can organize the orders table, the customers table, and the products table under a catalog. Next, we have Jobs and Pipelines. This is essentially a task scheduling center. Here, you can define a job. Job means running a specific task automatically at certain intervals. For instance, if you want to pull in new order data every morning at 8 a.m. and write it into a table, you can set that up as a job. Below the Jobs section, there is a feature called Delta Live Tables, D-L-T. Delta Live Tables is a method used for processing data. Here, an important concept comes up, ETL. ETL stands for extract, transform, load. In other words, extract. Take the raw data, for example, the orders table, transform, clean, and adjust it. Remove incorrect records, fix date formats and stuff. Load, put it into the target table, for example, reporting table. DLT is a modern tool that simplifies this ETL process. Now let's look at the Compute menu. Compute is the heart of Databricks. This is where you create clusters. A cluster is like a group of computers working for you. Instead of relying on a single machine, you combine the power of multiple machines to speed up your processing. For small datasets, you can set up a simple cluster. For large datasets, you can build a more powerful one. If you prefer, you can also choose the serverless option where Databricks manages all the infrastructure for you so you can focus only on your work. The Marketplace menu is a section where you can integrate external services. For example, you can connect tools like DBT, Data Build Tool, or Fivetran with Databricks. This way you can speed up your data workflows by leveraging ready-made solutions. So that was the main section. Now let's move on to the subsections. Here you'll find the SQL Editor. In this part, you can write SQL queries and analyze your tables. Let's say you wanna find out the total number of orders in the past week. You can write that query in the SQL Editor and get the result. In the Queries section, you can view your past queries. In the Dashboard section, you can visualize your query results and create panels to share with your managers. There is also an AI assistant called Genie. You can ask Genie a natural language question like, which city had the most orders in the past month? Genie will translate that into an SQL query for you and return the result. As from here, we move on to the Data Engineering tab. In this section, you'll again see parts like Jobs and Job Runs. But the most interesting one for us is the Data Ingestion menu. The word ingestion means bringing data in. From this menu, you can upload a CSV file from your computer or connect the cloud data source into Databricks. For example, if you have an Excel file containing a customer list, you can easily convert it into a table here and analyze it with SQL. All right, let's continue. At the very bottom, we have the AI slash ML menu. This section is about data science and machine learning. From here, you can build models, run experiments, and even deploy your models as services. Since the main focus of our course is data engineering, we won't dive too deeply into this, but it's useful to know that Databricks is an end-to-end platform. It doesn't just process data. It also supports AI projects. So that was the menu. Now let's move on to the middle section. Friends, at the top of the screen, you'll see a search bar. When you type into it, it scans all assets across your workspace. Let's say you have hundreds of files, but you're looking for a notebook named Test. Just type the name and search will instantly find it. You can also filter your search, for example, to look only for notebooks, only for tables, or only for jobs. In the middle of the screen, you'll see three important tiles. You might not see them later on since they're constantly being updated, but I'll explain them here for those who are curious. Converse with your data using natural language. Here, you can use Databricks AI-powered Genie feature to interact with your data in natural language. This means without writing SQL, you can ask a question like, what was the best seller product last month? And Databricks will give you the answer and even visualize it. This feature helps bridge the gap between business teams and data engineers. Explore data and share insights with notebooks. The second tile is about notebooks, Databricks most widely used tool. Here, you can explore data, write queries, visualize results, and even collaborate with your team in real time. In this course, we'll be using notebooks a lot. Build your first AI agent. The third option is about one of today's most popular fields, building AI agents. Here, you can create simple functions, turn them into an AI-powered agent, and use it to automatically generate insights from your data. These three features demonstrate how broad the vision of Databricks is. It's not just a platform for storing data. It's not just an environment for querying and processing. It's also a platform that delivers end-to-end data and AI solutions powered by artificial intelligence. All right, let's continue. Finally, let's talk about the profile menu in the top right corner. From here, you can adjust your user setting, such as switching between dark and light themes, enabling or disabling preview features, or editing your account information. If you have multiple workspaces, you can easily switch between them from here as well. For example, let's change the theme. Look, under the preferences section, you can choose either dark or light mode. I'll continue with the light theme.
