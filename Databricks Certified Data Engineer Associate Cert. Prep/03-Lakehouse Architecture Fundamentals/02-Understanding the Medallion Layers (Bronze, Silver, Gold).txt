Understanding the Medallion Layers, Bronze, Silver, Gold Layers Dear friends, hello everyone. As you know, in the previous lesson, I explained the lake house concept along with its history. Now we already understand what it is. One of the strongest aspects of the lake house architecture is that it can manage data in well-structured layers. This approach is called the medallion architecture. In this lesson, we will examine this approach in detail. Friends, the word medallion here is not a coincidence. It symbolizes how data gains value as it passes through each layer. Raw data, bronze is ingested, quality is improved, silver, and finally it becomes high-value business summaries, gold. It's just like mining. Ore is extracted, then refined, and eventually turned into a valuable medallion. Never forget this saying, what you hold in your hand may be just raw ore, but if refined, it becomes a jewel. Yes, this approach is the philosophy of layer design on the journey of turning data into a jewel. Now let's expand the idea a bit. Friends, the medallion architecture is not an infrastructure architecture, but rather a data design pattern. This means you don't need to install any special software to implement it. The important thing is the discipline of organizing data into layers. The reason we choose a layered structure is to make the data journey more transparent and more manageable. Just as a factory divides production into stages, raw material intake, processing, packaging, medallion divides data into layers. Raw, clean, business ready. So let's go through these layers one by one. Bronze layer, power of raw data. Friends, the bronze layer is the first stop for data. Here, data is stored exactly as it comes from the sources. Log files, IOT device streams, JSON, CSV images, videos, everything is kept directly in this layer. Almost no transformations are applied. Only additional metadata like load date or file name might be added. The advantage of this approach is that you never lose the data history. If in the future someone asks, was this data processed incorrectly? You can always go back to the bronze layer and reuse the original raw data. This increases the system's reliability. You can think of the bronze layer as the warehouse entry point where trucks drop off materials. The materials haven't been washed or sorted yet, but if you want to see the factory's full history, this is the most accurate record. Silver layer, cleaning and organizing. Friends, the silver layer is where the data gains quality. Here, data engineers take the raw data and put it through a series of cleaning steps. Missing values are filled, faulty records are removed, columns are standardized, duplicates are eliminated, and sometimes additional annotations or relationships are added to enrich the data. The most important feature of this layer is that it builds trust in the data. Now analysts and data scientists can confidently use it because it has a defined standard. In a metaphor, the silver layer is like the kitchen counter where vegetables are washed and chopped. Rotten tomatoes are thrown away, the rest are cleaned and sliced. It's not a finished meal yet, but it's ready to be cooked. Gold layer, transforming into business value. Friends, the gold layer is the one most used by the business world. Here, the clean data from the silver layer is summarized, joined, and made ready for business intelligence. For example, daily sales data is aggregated into weekly totals, customer data is segmented, KPI tables are produced. The data in this layer is used directly for reporting, dashboards, and executive decision-making. It is optimized for managers, not for data scientists. The gold layer is like the finished dishes served on elegant plates at the table. At this stage, everything is easy to consume, understandable, and highly valuable. Why do we separate into three layers? There are several key reasons. Traceability, lineage. You can track step-by-step where the data came from, how it was processed, and where it went. Quality management. Each layer ensures quality control. Raw data may be messy, but after silver, it becomes trustworthy. And in gold, it aligns with business logic. Performance. For massive data volumes, reprocessing everything every time is costly. With the medallion structure, only the new, incoming data is processed. Incremental processing. Security. Sensitive data can be stored in the bronze or silver layers, while broader access can be given only to the aggregated data in the gold layer. This way, who sees what data can be controlled more effectively? Who does what? Data engineer. Plays the most critical role in moving data from bronze to silver. They build the ETL slash ELT pipelines and code the data cleaning and transformation processes. Let's briefly explain ETL and ELT, since these two terms often come up when understanding the medallion architecture. They are the approaches used to move and transform data across the layer. ETL. Extract, transform, load. First, data is extracted from source systems. Extract. Then it is cleaned slash transformed. Transform. Finally, it is loaded into the target system. Load. In this case, the lake house. Example. Take sales data coming from a store. Filter out invalid records. Convert all currencies to TL and then load it into the silver layer. ELT. Extract, load, transform. Here, the data is first loaded into the target system. Load. And then transform inside that system. Transform. Example. Load all log files as is into bronze, then use spark code in Databricks to transform them into clean silver data. In summary, ETL. Clean outside or at the source, then load. ELT. Load first, then clean inside the lake house. In modern cloud-based systems like Databricks, ELT is usually preferred because the system is very powerful. It's faster to load the raw data first, then transform it internally, continuing with roles. Ada Stewart, data quality specialist, defines quality rules in the silver layer and ensures standards are applied. Data scientist. Takes the enriched data from the silver layer and develops machine learning or AI model. Data analyst slash BI developer. Uses the aggregated data in the gold layer to create reports and dashboard. Think of the process as a factory production line. Raw material is delivered by logistics. Data engineer. Quality control. Data steward. Inspects and filters it. Product development. Data scientist. Processes it. Sales slash marketing. Analyst slash BI developer. Delivers it to the customer. Final notes. Not every project must have all three layers. Some projects work with just two, for example, bronze plus gold. In some enterprise projects, an additional platinum layer is defined for special, highly critical business reports. What's important is to clearly define each layer's role from the start and apply the discipline consistently. Summary. The medallion architecture is one of the key building blocks of the lake house. It guides data on a structured journey. Bronze stores raw data. Silver improves data quality. Gold transforms it into business value for decision making. Thanks to this layered approach, data becomes traceable, trustworthy, and suitable for different roles. In short, medallion is the journey of turning data from raw ore into a medallion or jewel.
